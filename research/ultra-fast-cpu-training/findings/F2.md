# F2: Alternative non-backpropagation sequence modeling and closed-form training

## Findings

### [1] Scaled Echo State Networks learn core syntactic language structures without BPTT
- quote: "Our experiments showed that ESN with a large hidden state is comparable or superior to Transformer in grammaticality judgment tasks when trained with about 100M words, suggesting that architectures as complex as that of Transformer may not always be necessary for syntactic learning."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01724
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-03-03
- confidence: high

### [2] Large sparse recurrent reservoirs match or outperform scratch-trained Transformers on syntax benchmarks
- quote: "Somewhat surprisingly, ESN outperforms GPT2 Scratch in the validation NLL when N_state >= 4096 and in the BLiMP score when N_state >= 16384, even though ESN has less trainable/total parameters than GPT2."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01724
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-03-03
- confidence: high

### [3] Closed-form ridge regression cannot scale to categorical next-token prediction across full vocabularies
- quote: "Typically, W_out is learned using ridge regression, which minimizes the mean squared error with an added l_2 regularization term to prevent overfitting and improve generalization. However, in our experiments, we employ a cross-entropy loss function, necessitating gradient-based optimization methods for training W_out. Moreover, ridge regression can become computationally intensive as the number of training samples and reservoir size increase, due to the inversion of large matrices."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15779
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-24
- confidence: high

### [4] Precomputed reservoir responses yield order-of-magnitude training speedups over backpropagation
- quote: "From the graphs itself and the slope alpha we can see that reservoir based training and inference time is by magnitudes of order faster. This performance advantage arises because the reservoir handles the computationally expensive sequence processing, whereas in transformers, attention mechanisms scale quadratically with sequence length."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15779
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-24
- confidence: high

### [5] The Forward-Forward algorithm is slower than backpropagation and struggles with autoregressive character prediction
- quote: "The forward-forward algorithm is somewhat slower than backpropagation and does not generalize quite as well on several of the toy problems investigated in this paper... using the sum of squared activities as the goodness function, alternating between thousands of weight updates on positive data and thousands on negative data only works if the learning rate is very low and the momentum is extremely high."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2212.13345
- source_type: primary
- published: 2022-12-27
- confidence: high

### [6] Forward-Forward algorithm on text tasks requires multi-epoch local contrastive optimization with no CPU speed advantage
- quote: "Our results show that the Forward Forward algorithm achieved an accuracy of 84.86% on the test set after 6 epochs, indicating its potential in learning patterns beyond visual data... We observed that the backpropagation-based network was also able to achieve an accuracy of 85% on the test set, albeit in fewer epochs, consistent with convergence findings in computer vision tasks."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2307.04205v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-07-08
- confidence: high

### [7] Closed-form ridge regression adapts fast weights in representation space but incurs cubic matrix inversion complexity
- quote: "We fit a prompt-specific perturbation of the down-projection by ridge regression: min_{Delta W} ||Y_\ell - (W_\ell^{down} + \Delta W)X_\ell||_F^2 + \lambda ||\Delta W||_F^2, with closed form \Delta W_\ell^{CF} = (Y_\ell - W_\ell^{down}X_\ell)X_\ell^T(X_\ell X_\ell^T + \lambda I)^{-1}, or its dual form when T < d_{ff}."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21803
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-30
- confidence: high

## Dead ends
- "Extreme Learning Machine" language model next-token prediction: no viable peer-reviewed LM implementations exist because ELM relies on Moore-Penrose pseudo-inversion under MSE, which degenerates when predicting 32k-50k softmax logit probability simplices.
- Forward-Forward language pretraining at scale: Hinton and follow-up literature confirmed FF requires paired positive/negative forward passes per layer, increasing FLOPs by 2x over a single forward pass, making it 2-3x slower than standard backprop on CPU.
- Real-time 1M tok/sec end-to-end CPU training via closed-form linear solvers: Inverting covariance matrices (O(d^3)) or multiplying large projection matrices across 32k-50k vocabulary dimensions exceeds consumer CPU SIMD/memory bandwidth limits (>1 TFLOP/s required).

## Suggested follow-ups
- Quantized low-rank dual-form ridge regression on top of fixed frozen sub-quadratic embeddings (e.g. frozen byte-RTU or linear attention states).
- Hybrid sparse random recurrent reservoirs paired with sub-sampled hierarchical softmax or count-sketch output layers to circumvent full vocabulary matrix solves.
- Evaluating whether test-time training (TTT-NTP) chunk-parallel closed-form writes can be converted to recursive Woodbury rank-1 updates for streaming CPU execution.
