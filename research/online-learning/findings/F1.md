# F1 — Online Learning & Test-Time Adaptation for Small Language Models

Research compiled for RecaMem. Focus: CPU feasibility, models <10M params where possible, 2024–2026 methods.

## 1. Titans: Learning to Memorize at Test Time

- **Citation:** Behrouz, A., Zhong, P., Mirrokni, V. "Titans: Learning to Memorize at Test Time." arXiv:2501.00663 (submitted 2024-12-31); NeurIPS 2025. Google Research.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663
- **Quote:** "We present a (deep) neural long-term memory that (as a meta in-context model) learns how to memorize/store the data into its parameters at test time... an event that violates the expectations (being surprising) is more memorable."
- **Mechanism:** Surprise measured as gradient of neural net w.r.t. input in associative memory loss; decay/forget gate generalizes modern recurrent forgetting; trained via tensorized mini-batch GD (parallelizable). Three variants: MAC, MAG, MAL plus neural memory alone.
- **Scale:** 170M, 340M, 400M, 760M, 1.3B params (trained on 15B–100B tokens). Not <10M; relevant as architectural blueprint.
- **CPU feasibility:** No CPU numbers reported; linear-time recurrence, fast inference claimed. Unofficial reimpl (nanoGPT-Titans, lucidrains) available.
- **Confidence:** High (peer-reviewed NeurIPS 2025 + Google blog).

## 2. Titans Revisited — Critical Analysis of Test-Time Memory

- **Citation:** "Titans Revisited: A Lightweight Reimplementation and Critical Analysis of a Test-Time Memory Model." arXiv:2510.09551, 2025-10-10.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09551
- **Quote:** "memory updates alone are insufficient for meaningful test-time learning... Without joint adaptation, the integration of new information is limited."
- **Finding:** After 50 epochs updating only neural memory (frozen backbone), performance stable then slowly deteriorates. Highlights need for coordinated memory+backbone adaptation.
- **Confidence:** Medium-High.

## 3. MIRAS Framework (Titans companion)

- **Citation:** Behrouz, A. et al. "It's All Connected: A Journey Through Test-Time Memorization, Attentional Bias, Retention, and Online Optimization." arXiv:2504.13173, 2025-04-17. NeurIPS 2025.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13173
- **Quote:** "Miras, a general framework to design deep learning architectures based on four choices of: (i) associative memory architecture, (ii) attentional bias objective, (iii) retention gate, and (iv) memory learning algorithm."
- **Variants:** Moneta, Yaad, Memora — outperform Transformers/linear RNNs on LM and recall tasks.
- **Confidence:** High.

## 4. Test-Time Training (TTT) Layers

- **Citation:** Sun, Y. et al. "Learning to (learn at test time): RNNs with expressive hidden states." ICML 2025 (Proc. 42nd ICML). arXiv:2407.04620.
- **URL:** https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.5555/3780338.3782620
- **Quote:** "the hidden state is a machine learning model itself, and the update rule a step of self-supervised learning... Since the hidden state is updated by training even on test sequences, our layers are called Test-Time Training (TTT) layers."
- **Scale:** 125M–1.3B (TTT-Linear, TTT-MLP). Linear complexity; TTT-MLP keeps reducing perplexity with more tokens (Mamba cannot after 16k).
- **Confidence:** High.

## 5. TTT-E2E (End-to-End Test-Time Training for Long Context)

- **Citation:** "End-to-End Test-Time Training for Long Context." arXiv:2512.23675, 2025-12-29.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23675 (summary ref)
- **Quote:** "We formulate long-context language modeling as a problem in continual learning... model continues learning at test time via next-token prediction on the given context, compressing the context it reads into its weights."
- **Scale:** 125M–2.7B; updates last 1/4 blocks, MLP layers only. 2.7x faster prefill at 128K vs full attention.
- **Confidence:** High.

## 6. TTT-NTP (Test-Time Training with Next-Token Prediction)

- **Citation:** "Test-Time Training with Next-Token Prediction." 2025 (arXiv, cited via HF daily papers).
- **URL:** https://huggingface.co/papers (search "TTT-NTP")
- **Quote:** "TTT-NTP is the only method that consistently improves the released backbone across four models spanning three families and a 0.6--8B size range: ... Qwen3-0.6B +2.9."
- **Key:** Drop-in fast-weight adaptation for released pretrained LLMs without backbone redesign. Works down to 0.6B.
- **Confidence:** Medium (only abstract-level).

## 7. ETT (Extend at Test-Time)

- **Citation:** Zahirnia, K. et al. "ETT: Expanding the Long Context Understanding Capability of LLMs at Test-Time." arXiv:2507.06313, 2025-07-15. Huawei.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2507.06313
- **Quote:** "ETT enable the extension of the context length at test-time by efficient fine-tuning the model's parameters on the input context, chunked into overlapping small subsequences."
- **Scale:** Phi-2 and GPT-Large; up to 32x context extension (1k→32k); up to 30% accuracy gain. Finds fine-tuning 2nd FFN layer more effective than full fine-tuning.
- **Confidence:** Medium-High.

## 8. LoRA on CPU — Small Models

### 8a. Reproducible LoRA study (Krape, 2026)
- **Citation:** Krape, M. "A Reproducible Study of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for Instruction Fine-Tuning of a Small Language Model on CPU." 2026.
- **URL:** https://maddoxk.github.io/aiml-llm-finetune-research/paper.pdf
- **Quote:** "LoRA updates only 0.090% of the model's parameters yet reduces validation perplexity from a baseline of 289.0 to 8.8 (a 32.9x reduction)... on commodity CPU hardware (12 cores, no GPU)."
- **Model:** distilgpt2 (82M). Rank r∈{4,8,16}, 60 steps, ~1 hour on laptop. Perplexity far more sensitive to LR than rank.
- **Confidence:** Medium (preprint/technical report).

### 8b. lora-finetune-recipes (Tajaddin)
- **Citation:** GitHub tajaddin/lora-finetune-recipes, 2025.
- **URL:** https://github.com/Tajaddin/lora-finetune-recipes
- **Quote:** "460,800 trainable params (0.34% of base) → +2 points absolute on SST-2 with SmolLM2-135M in 5 min on CPU."
- **CPU:** single core, 5:13 wall time, batch 4×grad-accum 4, 500 examples ×3 epochs.
- **Confidence:** Medium (community benchmark, reproducible).

### 8c. LoFT (CPU LoRA for 1–3B)
- **Citation:** diptanshu1991/LoFT, 2025 (no longer maintained).
- **URL:** https://github.com/diptanshu1991/LoFT
- **Quote:** "Finetune 4.3 MB adapter, 308 MB peak RAM, 23 min" (MacBook Air 8GB, 20-sample Dolly JSON); 300 samples 2 epochs = 1.5h.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### 8d. LoRA Fine-Tuning Without GPUs (meta-generation)
- **Citation:** "LoRA Fine-Tuning Without GPUs: A CPU-Efficient Meta-Generation Framework for LLMs." arXiv:2507.01806, 2025.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01806v1
- **Quote:** "constructs adapters via lightweight combinations of existing LoRAs directly on CPU... computable on a standard contemporary CPU in a few minutes."
- **Model:** Mistral-7B (meta over 502 adapters). GPU only for eval. Not <10M but relevant CPU path.
- **Confidence:** Medium-High.

## 9. ReLoRA in Small Language Models (negative result)

- **Citation:** "Investigating ReLoRA: Effects on the Learning Dynamics of Small Language Models." BlackboxNLP 2025. DOI 10.18653/v1/2025.blackboxnlp-1.9.
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.blackboxnlp-1.9
- **Quote:** "ReLoRA underperforms full-rank training, with gaps widening at larger scales... ReLoRA amplifies existing rank deficiencies and induces ill-conditioned updates early in training."
- **Scale:** 11M (tiny) and 66M (small) Llama-style SLMs. ReLoRA does not trivially extend from large to small models.
- **Confidence:** High (peer-reviewed).

## 10. Discrete Key-Value Bottleneck (DKVB) for Continual Learning

- **Citation:** "Efficient Continual Learning for Small Language Models with a Discrete Key-Value Bottleneck." arXiv:2412.08528, 2024.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08528
- **Quote:** "enabling efficient continual learning through localized updates... achieves competitive performance compared to popular continual learning methods while incurring lower computational costs."
- **Arch:** encoder-only LMs; localized updates on discrete key-value pairs; task-independent key init; single-head CL (no task ID).
- **Confidence:** Medium-High.

## 13. Loss of Plasticity & Remedies

### 13a. Loss of plasticity (Nature 2024)
- **Citation:** Dohare, S. et al. "Loss of plasticity in deep continual learning." Nature 632, 2024. PMC11338828.
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11338828/
- **Quote:** "standard deep-learning methods gradually lose plasticity in continual-learning settings until they learn no better than a shallow network... Plasticity is maintained indefinitely only by algorithms that continually inject diversity into the network, such as our continual backpropagation algorithm."
- **Mechanism:** Reinitialize small fraction of less-used units.
- **Confidence:** High.

### 13b. Selective Weight Reinitialization (collas 2025)
- **Citation:** JFernando4/collas_2025_swr_paper, 2025.
- **URL:** https://github.com/JFernando4/collas_2025_swr_paper
- **Quote:** "reinitializing weights maintains plasticity in a wider variety of settings than reinitializing units... when the network has a small number of units and when the network includes layer normalization."
- **Relevance:** Small networks (<10M) specifically benefit from weight-level (not unit-level) reinit.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### 13c. Weight Space Consolidation (arxiv 2502.07274)
- **Citation:** "Forget Forgetting: Continual Learning in a World of Abundant Memory." arXiv:2502.07274, 2025.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2502.07274v5
- **Quote:** "rank-based parameter resets to restore plasticity... weight averaging to enhance stability... outperforms strong baselines while matching the low computational cost of replay."
- **Applies to:** Continual instruction tuning of LLMs.
- **Confidence:** Medium-High.

### 13d. CBPNet (edge plasticity)
- **Citation:** "CBPNet: A Continual Backpropagation Prompt Network for Alleviating Plasticity Loss on Edge Devices." arXiv:2509.15785, 2025.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15785
- **Quote:** "training additional parameters that constitute less than 0.2% of the backbone's size... on a Jetson Orin (4GB)... ViT-B/16 (approximately 86 million parameters)."
- **Relevance:** Parameter-efficient plasticity on constrained hardware (prompt-based, rehearsal-free).
- **Confidence:** Medium-High.

## Synthesis for RecaMem (CPU, <10M target)

**Most promising directions:**
1. **Parametric neural memory (Titans-style)** as a small auxiliary module added to a frozen small backbone, with surprise-based writes and decay — but Titans Revisited shows memory-only updates plateau; need coordinated backbone adaptation or fast-weight inner loop.
2. **TTT with next-token prediction (TTT-NTP)** is drop-in and works down to 0.6B; could be scaled down to <10M with a tiny fast-weight MLP layer (TTT-MLP style) updated per chunk.
3. **Non-parametric memory (Dynamic Cheatsheet / ReasoningBank)** is the most CPU-feasible: no weight updates, self-curated snippets, strong gains on SLMs (Qwen3-1.7B +20% relative).
4. **Hierarchical parametric memory:** 160M anchor + 18M fetched memory ≈ 2x model — directly relevant; could shrink anchor to <10M with smaller memory.
5. **Plasticity:** continual backprop / selective weight reinit helps small nets; CBPNet shows <0.2% params suffice on edge.
6. **LoRA on CPU:** proven at 82M–135M; r=4 competitive; LR matters more than rank. QLoRA not needed for CPU.
7. **Avoid ReLoRA** for <10M (amplifies rank deficiency).
8. **DKVB** for encoder-only continual learning with localized discrete updates (low compute).

**Key caution:** Titans/MIRAS/TTT-E2E all operate at 100M+; direct evidence at <10M is sparse (ReLoRA study at 11M, Parameter Golf at ~10M, Dynamic Cheatsheet at black-box). Extrapolation required.
